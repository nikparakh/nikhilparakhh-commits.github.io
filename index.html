01 · Professional · SFV Services / ROARK Design · 2026

Building a clearer content system for a technical design brand

My role
Recurring calendar ownership, content strategy, copywriting, visual production, review coordination

ROARK needed LinkedIn content that could explain the value of commercial architecture without relying on generic design language or repeating the same service claims.

I built each 12–16-post cycle around the moments when design decisions become operational decisions: peak-hour traffic, first-time wayfinding, maintenance demands, storage, acoustics, and employee workflow. That framing made technical expertise easier for a business audience to recognize.

Approach

Created practical content pillars, matched each topic to a specific audience question, and used restrained calls to action instead of forcing a sales pitch into every post.

Execution

Wrote LinkedIn copy, produced branded visuals, requested service and project context from leadership, incorporated review notes, and prepared multiple cycles for publishing in Loomly.

Outcome

A repeatable content system that met recurring deadlines and earned positive stakeholder feedback for imagery, consistency, and website-directed calls to action.

03 · Professional · SFV Services / LienNow · 2026

Making payment-protection content useful, not alarmist

My role
Content development, copywriting, visual production, revisions, publishing preparation

Construction payment protection is deadline-driven and technical. The content had to create urgency without making legal promises or overwhelming contractors with process language.

I organized the campaign around recognizable working moments: a new job, scope changes, delivery records, overdue invoices, closeout, and the information needed to start.

Insight

The clearest content began with the contractor's task or pressure, then connected it to documentation and payment protection.

Iteration

Reworked the content into a tighter two-part structure, clarified that LienNow is a payment-protection service rather than project-management software, and added a standard CTA.

Outcome

The revised September batch—10 new posts plus a Labor Day post—received final leadership approval for publishing and moved into the Loomly workflow.

  • B2B marketing
  • Complex-topic simplification
  • Copywriting
  • Content systems
  • Brand consistency

05 · Academic team case analysis · 2025

Closing the gap between sustainability work and consumer trust

My contribution
Consumer-perception analysis and final synthesis

Perception gap

Consumers may not know the work or may doubt the claim.

Brand tension

Sustainability can be perceived as a tradeoff with performance.

Recommendation

Connect environmental progress to product benefits and show evidence.

Nike's Considered initiative showed a classic brand problem: operational progress does not automatically change consumer perception.

My section focused on the trust gap and the risk that customers would interpret sustainability as a compromise in performance or quality.

Insight

For a performance-led brand, sustainability communication is stronger when it explains how material choices improve the product—not only the planet.

Recommendation

Use specific proof, product demonstrations, and targeted communication instead of broad sustainability claims.

Scope

This was a team assignment. I owned the consumer-perception analysis and contributed the conclusion; I do not present the full paper as individual work.
