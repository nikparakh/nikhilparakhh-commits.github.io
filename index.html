01 · Professional · SFV Services / ROARK Design · 2026

Building a clearer content system for a technical design brand

My role
Content strategy, copywriting, visual direction, Canva production, review coordination

ROARK needed LinkedIn content that could explain the value of commercial architecture without relying on generic design language or repeating the same service claims.

I built the content around the moments when design decisions become operational decisions: peak-hour traffic, first-time wayfinding, maintenance demands, storage, acoustics, and employee workflow. That framing made technical expertise easier for a business audience to recognize.

Approach

Created practical content pillars, matched each topic to a specific audience question, and used restrained calls to action instead of forcing a sales pitch into every post.

Execution

Wrote long-form LinkedIn copy, shaped visual concepts and revisions, produced branded materials in Canva, and kept the voice consistent across monthly content.

Outcome

A repeatable content library that shows ROARK's expertise through concrete business and user situations. The examples shown are a focused selection from the broader campaign.

02 · Professional · SFV Services · 2026

One campaign. Three distinct brand voices.

My role
Campaign messaging, copywriting, visual direction, cross-brand quality review

A shared partner-recruitment campaign had to work across architecture, renewable energy, and commercial real estate without flattening three companies into the same voice.

A shared visual structure was not enough. Each brand needed its own reason to believe: coordination for ROARK, qualifications and coverage for REIN, and lifecycle expertise for New Market.

Approach

Built a shared message architecture around capabilities, standards, fit, and a unified application path, then changed the proof points and vocabulary by brand.

Quality control

Audited copy-to-visual alignment and removed unsupported claims about guaranteed work, project volume, priority access, and automatic approval.

Deliverable

A 24-post campaign: eight pieces for each brand, ordered for publication and aligned to one enrollment destination.

03 · Professional · SFV Services / LienNow · 2026

Making payment-protection content useful, not alarmist

My role
Content development, copywriting, visual direction, branded communications

Construction payment protection is deadline-driven and technical. The content had to create urgency without making legal promises or overwhelming contractors with process language.

I organized the campaign around recognizable working moments: a new job, scope changes, delivery records, overdue invoices, closeout, and the information needed to start.

Insight

The clearest content began with the contractor's task or pressure, then connected it to documentation and payment protection.

Execution

Developed a ten-post campaign with concise visual headlines and fuller LinkedIn captions, supported by a consistent navy, white, and red brand system.

Outcome

A coherent campaign that translated a specialized service into practical, audience-focused messages without inventing results or guarantees.

  • B2B marketing
  • Complex-topic simplification
  • Copywriting
  • Content systems
  • Brand consistency

05 · Academic team case analysis · 2025

Closing the gap between sustainability work and consumer trust

My contribution
Consumer-perception analysis and final synthesis

Perception gap

Consumers may not know the work or may doubt the claim.

Brand tension

Sustainability can be perceived as a tradeoff with performance.

Recommendation

Connect environmental progress to product benefits and show evidence.

Nike's Considered initiative showed a classic brand problem: operational progress does not automatically change consumer perception.

My section focused on the trust gap and the risk that customers would interpret sustainability as a compromise in performance or quality.

Insight

For a performance-led brand, sustainability communication is stronger when it explains how material choices improve the product—not only the planet.

Recommendation

Use specific proof, product demonstrations, and targeted communication instead of broad sustainability claims.

Scope

This was a team assignment. I owned the consumer-perception analysis and contributed the conclusion; I do not present the full paper as individual work.
